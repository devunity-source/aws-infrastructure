Every few months someone on our team asks which MacBook to buy and the answer is always "it depends," which is useless. So here is what it depends on, in the order that matters. If you want the short version, scroll to the bottom. If you want the ranked list across brands, that is in our [best laptops for developers](/best-laptops-for-developers) guide.

## The honest summary

The MacBook Air is a very fast laptop with no fan. The MacBook Pro is the same family of chips with a fan, a much better screen, more ports, and the option of the Pro and Max chips that the Air cannot have.

For most developers, most of the time, the Air is enough and the money saved buys a monitor. For a specific set of workloads, the Pro is not a luxury. Let us be specific about which.

## Where the Air wins

**Weight and silence.** The Air is lighter and makes no noise, ever. If you work in libraries, cafes, or an open office, that matters every day.

**Price.** The gap between a well-configured Air and a comparable Pro is the price of a very good external monitor, or a year of cloud compute for side projects.

**Battery.** Both are excellent. The Air's lower-power design and smaller screen often give it the edge in a day of mixed work.

**Enough performance for most work.** Web development, mobile development, scripting, data analysis in notebooks, cloud consoles, infrastructure-as-code, writing, and the editor and browser that go with all of it. The base chip in an Air handles all of that without a hint of strain.

## Where the Pro wins

**Sustained load.** The Air has no fan. Under a long compile, a large test suite, a video export or a local model run, it gets warm and slows down to protect itself. The Pro keeps going at full speed indefinitely. If your day involves builds that run longer than a few minutes, several times a day, the Pro pays for itself in waiting time.

**Memory ceiling.** The Air tops out lower than the Pro. If you need a lot of memory for virtual machines, large datasets, or local language models, only the Pro with a Pro or Max chip gets you there.

**The screen.** The Pro's mini-LED panel is brighter, has far better contrast, and refreshes at 120 Hz. Scrolling code is smoother. Dark themes look black instead of grey. Working near a window is comfortable. If the laptop screen is your main screen, this is the biggest daily difference between the two.

**Ports.** The Pro has three Thunderbolt ports, HDMI, an SD card slot and MagSafe. The Air has two USB-C ports and MagSafe. The Pro drives more external displays. The Air with a base chip supports fewer.

**GPU work.** Anything that leans on the GPU, from machine learning to video, scales with the Pro and Max chips in a way the Air cannot match.

## Match the machine to the work

**Front-end, full-stack web, scripting, cloud and infrastructure work.** Air. Get 16 GB minimum, 24 GB if you run Docker all day. Spend the difference on a good monitor and a dock.

**Mobile development.** Air for small to medium apps. Pro if you work on a large Xcode project where clean builds take minutes, because the fan is the difference between a two-minute build and a four-minute one.

**Backend with heavy local services.** Pro with 32 GB or more. A database, a message queue, a few services and a Kubernetes cluster will use it.

**Data science and local machine learning.** Pro with a Pro or Max chip and as much unified memory as you can justify. Memory is the limit for models, and the GPU cores matter.

**Video, audio or 3D alongside code.** Pro. The screen alone decides it.

**Travel-heavy consulting.** Air, unless one of the above applies. Weight wins over a thousand airport walks.

## Screen size

The Air comes in 13 and 15 inches. The Pro in 14 and 16. The 15-inch Air is the sleeper pick: a big screen in a light body for people who do not need the Pro's thermals. The 16-inch Pro is a desktop replacement and it feels like one in a bag.

## Memory, one more time

Whichever you choose, memory is soldered and the choice is permanent. 16 GB is the floor. 24 or 32 GB is where developers should land. Nobody has ever wished they had bought less.

## The short version

- Most developers: MacBook Air, 16 to 24 GB, and a good monitor.
- Long builds several times a day: MacBook Pro 14 with a Pro chip.
- Local models, large data, VMs: MacBook Pro with a Pro or Max chip and 48 GB or more.
- Laptop screen is your main screen: MacBook Pro, for the display alone.
- You travel constantly and none of the above applies: MacBook Air 13.
