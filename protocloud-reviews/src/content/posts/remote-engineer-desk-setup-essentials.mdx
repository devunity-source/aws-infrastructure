Most people set up a home office in a hurry and never revisited it. Laptop on a desk, maybe a monitor, the webcam the laptop came with, and then years passed. This is the list of upgrades we would make first, the ones that pay for themselves in comfort, in how you come across on calls, or just in reclaimed desk space.

## The order to buy in

If you can only do one thing, get the monitor arm. It changes the ergonomics of the whole desk. After that, the webcam and microphone, because that is how your colleagues and clients experience you. The rest is quality of life.

<ProductCard index={0} {...frontmatter.products[0]} />

A monitor arm lifts the screen off the desk and lets you put it exactly at eye height, then pull it closer when you lean in or push it away when you stand. Ergotron's LX has been the default for over a decade. The gas spring stays where you leave it, the cable channel hides the mess, and the warranty runs ten years.

Check your monitor's weight. Big 40-inch ultrawides need the heavy-duty version.

<ProductCard index={1} {...frontmatter.products[1]} />

Laptop webcams have improved and are still bad. The Insta360 Link 2 puts a 4K sensor on a tiny gimbal that follows you around the room, frames you automatically, and points itself at the desk when you are done, so it doubles as a privacy shutter. Reviewers comparing it with built-in cameras agree the difference is not subtle.

If you never move during calls, the gimbal is a party trick. Logitech's MX Brio is the still-camera alternative at a similar level. Our [webcam guide](/blog/best-webcams-for-remote-work) compares five.

Audio is where people notice quality more than they realise. Laptop mics pick up the room, the keyboard and the dishwasher. The Shure MV7+ is a dynamic microphone, which means it only hears what is close to it. Reviewers say it sounds like a studio even in an untreated spare bedroom. The built-in denoiser handles the rest.

It needs a boom arm to sit within a hand's width of your mouth, so budget for one. Cheaper options are in our [USB microphone guide](/blog/best-usb-microphones-for-calls). It also has XLR, so when you outgrow USB it becomes the first piece of a proper setup rather than a dead end.

<ProductCard index={3} {...frontmatter.products[3]} />

A monitor light bar clips onto the top of the screen and throws light down onto the desk and keyboard without putting a single reflection on the panel. The Halo 2 also lights the wall behind the monitor, which softens the contrast between a bright screen and a dark room and, according to BenQ, reduces eye fatigue late in the day. The wireless dial lives on the desk and makes adjustment a single turn.

It costs more than a lamp. It also replaces a lamp, frees the space the lamp occupied, and fixes a problem the lamp caused.

<ProductCard index={4} {...frontmatter.products[4]} />

A Stream Deck is fifteen tiny screens that do whatever you tell them. For an engineer that might mean a mute button and a camera toggle that work in every meeting app, a button that opens the right tmux session, and one that runs the deploy script. The per-app profiles mean the keys change when you switch from Slack to VS Code.

The software is bigger than it should be and it is tempting to keep configuring forever. Set up five buttons and stop.

<ProductCard index={5} {...frontmatter.products[5]} />

If your laptop sits open next to a monitor, its screen is probably a foot lower than the monitor and you are looking down at it all day. The Curve Flex raises it to eye level and tilts it toward you. It folds flat, so it goes in the bag for the coworking days.

<ProductCard index={6} {...frontmatter.products[6]} />

Three charging cables for a phone, watch and earbuds become one cube that folds up and goes in a bag. The phone sits at an angle where notifications are glanceable. It is Apple-specific. Android users want a single Qi2 stand instead.

## What we left off

Standing desks are great and too personal to recommend by model. Chairs, the same. Ring lights make everyone look like a dentist's waiting room; a light bar and a window do better. Mechanical keyboards and mice have their own guides on this site.

## The short version

- Start with the Ergotron LX arm.
- Fix how you look: Insta360 Link 2.
- Fix how you sound: Shure MV7+.
- Fix your eyes at 9 pm: BenQ ScreenBar Halo 2.
- Fix the mute button panic: Stream Deck MK.2.
- Get the laptop screen to eye level: Twelve South Curve Flex.
- Charge your phone and watch in one place: Anker 3-in-1 MagSafe Cube.
