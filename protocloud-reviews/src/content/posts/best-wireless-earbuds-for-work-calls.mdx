Earbud reviews obsess over sound and noise cancelling and spend one sentence on the microphone. For work, the microphone is the product. If the person on the other end has to ask you to repeat yourself, nothing else matters. We tested these five on calls from a busy street, a train, and a windy platform, then judged the rest. For over-ear focus headphones, see our [noise-cancelling guide](/best-noise-cancelling-headphones-for-focus).

## What we tested

**Outgoing voice quality.** Recorded on the far end of a real call in three environments. Clean office, traffic, and wind. Wind is where most earbuds fall apart.

**Multipoint.** Connected to a laptop and a phone at once, taking a call on whichever rings. Not all earbuds do this well.

**Fit over a long day.** Pressure, slip, and whether they survive a walk between meetings.

**ANC on a commute.** Secondary, but a quiet commute is the other half of why people buy these.

<ProductCard index={0} {...frontmatter.products[0]} />

If you carry an iPhone and work on a Mac, the AirPods Pro 3 are the obvious choice and the microphone is the reason. Apple's voice isolation pulls your voice out of a noisy street better than anything else here. Pairing is instant, switching between devices is automatic, and the new foam-infused tips seal well enough that the noise cancelling rivals over-ear headphones.

On Android they lose most of the smart features and become expensive, good earbuds. The battery is a few hours per charge, so the case lives in your pocket.

<ProductCard index={1} {...frontmatter.products[1]} />

Sony's XM5 buds are small, light, and have the best noise cancelling on this list for non-Apple users. Call quality is strong, helped by a bone-conduction sensor that picks up your voice through your jaw, which keeps you clear in wind when ordinary mics struggle.

Touch controls are easy to trigger by accident. Fit depends on finding the right tip, and Sony includes several. Once dialled in, they are excellent for a commute and for calls.

Jabra builds headsets for call centres and that heritage shows. The Elite 8 Active Gen 2 has six microphones tuned for voice in bad conditions, and on the windy platform it was the clearest of the group. They are fully waterproof and built to be dropped. Multipoint between laptop and phone is the most reliable we tested.

Noise cancelling is a step below Apple and Sony and the buds are a bit bulkier. If calls on the move are the whole job, these are the ones.

<ProductCard index={3} {...frontmatter.products[3]} />

Samsung's Buds3 Pro are the pick for Galaxy phone owners. The stem design puts the microphones closer to your mouth, call quality is clear, and the pinch-and-swipe controls on the stem are precise. Sound is natural and noise cancelling is good.

Some of the best features only work with Samsung devices. People with smaller ears found the fit average. On a Galaxy phone with a Windows laptop they are a very good daily pair.

<ProductCard index={4} {...frontmatter.products[4]} />

Nothing's Ear (a) proves you do not need to spend much to get noise cancelling, multipoint and a long battery. The sound is lively, the design is fun, and the app is simple. For the price they are hard to fault.

Call quality is acceptable rather than impressive, and the plastic feels like the price. For a backup pair, a gym pair, or a first pair, they are the easy recommendation.

## Earbuds or a headset?

For an eight-hour day of back-to-back calls, a lightweight headset with a boom mic still beats any earbud for clarity and comfort. Earbuds win for everything else: the commute, the walk between meetings, the quick call from a cafe. Most of us own both.

## The short version

- iPhone and Mac: AirPods Pro 3.
- Android, best ANC: Sony WF-1000XM5.
- Calls in wind, rain and chaos: Jabra Elite 8 Active Gen 2.
- Samsung phone: Galaxy Buds3 Pro.
- Budget: Nothing Ear (a).
