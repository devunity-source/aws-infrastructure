Text is the whole job. A monitor for coding needs to render small, sharp glyphs for ten hours without fatigue, fit as many lines as your eyes can handle, and ideally charge your laptop over one cable so you never think about docks. Here are the five we would buy today.

## The three numbers that matter

**Pixel density.** Around 160 pixels per inch and up is where text stops looking like text and starts looking like print. A 27-inch 4K panel lands at 163 ppi. A 32-inch 4K drops to 138 and you will see it in thin fonts. Apple's 5K at 218 ppi is the gold standard.

**Vertical pixels.** Code is tall. More vertical space means more lines without scrolling. A 3:2 or a 16:10 panel gives you that. An ultrawide gives you two full editors side by side.

**Connectivity.** Thunderbolt or USB-C with enough power to charge your laptop turns the monitor into the dock. One cable in, everything on the desk lights up.

Refresh rate matters less than for gaming, but 120 Hz does make scrolling through a long file noticeably smoother.

Dell's IPS Black panels roughly double the contrast of a normal IPS, so dark editor themes look dark instead of grey. At 27 inches and 4K the text is crisp. The 120 Hz refresh is a quality-of-life upgrade over the 60 Hz standard. And the back is a full dock: Thunderbolt 4 with 140 W to charge a hungry laptop, Ethernet, a KVM for switching between two machines.

It is not cheap for the size. The stand is adequate. Mount it on an arm and it is close to perfect.

<ProductCard index={1} {...frontmatter.products[1]} />

BenQ built a monitor specifically for programmers and, unusually, it is not a gimmick. The 3:2 aspect ratio at 3840 by 2560 gives you 400 more vertical pixels than a 4K panel at the same width. In practice that is around fifteen extra lines of code on screen. The matte finish handles bright rooms well, and the "coding mode" is a tuned contrast and colour profile that genuinely helps dark themes read clearly.

It is 60 Hz and the aspect ratio makes video letterboxed. If you watch films on your work monitor, that will annoy you. If you write code on it, you will wonder why every monitor is not this shape.

A 40-inch 5K2K ultrawide is exactly two 27-inch 4K monitors glued together, minus the bezel in the middle. Same pixel density, so text is just as sharp. 120 Hz. Thunderbolt 4 with 140 W. Editor on the left, browser and terminal on the right, with room to spare.

You need a deep desk, because the panel is wide and curved. You need a tiling window manager or at least a snapping tool, because dragging windows across this much space gets old. And you need the budget. For people who already run two monitors, it is a straight upgrade.

<ProductCard index={3} {...frontmatter.products[3]} />

If you work on a Mac and you want the laptop screen and the external screen to look identical, this is the one. 218 ppi matches the MacBook exactly, so nothing changes size when you drag a window across. The webcam, microphones and speakers are good enough to retire three separate accessories.

It is 60 Hz, the height-adjustable stand is a paid upgrade, and connectivity is Thunderbolt only. On Windows it works but you lose brightness control and the camera features. Buy the nano-texture version if your office has a window behind you.

Not everyone needs IPS Black and 120 Hz. LG's UltraFine 4K gives you the same sharp 163 ppi text, USB-C with laptop charging, and a VESA mount, for a lot less than the Dell. Contrast is lower, so blacks are a bit grey in a dark room, and there are fewer ports.

For a second monitor, a home office on a budget, or a junior engineer's first real setup, it is the right call.

## Two monitors or one big one?

Two 27-inch 4K monitors cost less than one 40-inch ultrawide and give you the same pixels. The ultrawide wins on the missing bezel and the single cable. Two monitors win on flexibility, because you can rotate one to portrait for a log viewer or a long file. We have both camps on the team. Neither is wrong.

## The short version

- Just buy the good one: Dell U2725QE.
- You want the most lines of code on screen: BenQ RD280U.
- Replace your dual-monitor setup: Dell U4025QW.
- Mac, and you want it to feel like Apple built the desk: Studio Display.
- Sharp text without the premium: LG UltraFine 27-inch 4K.
