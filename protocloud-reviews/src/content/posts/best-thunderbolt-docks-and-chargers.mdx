The dream desk has one cable. You sit down, plug a single connector into the laptop, and the monitors, keyboard, Ethernet, webcam and charger all light up. The reality is a market full of docks that drop a display after sleep or charge the laptop too slowly to keep up with a build. These are the ones that have behaved on our desks.

## Thunderbolt 4 or 5?

Thunderbolt 4 at 40 Gbps is plenty for two 4K displays, 2.5 gigabit Ethernet and a handful of peripherals. Thunderbolt 5 doubles that and lets a dock drive three 4K or two 6K monitors with bandwidth to spare, plus faster external SSDs. If your laptop has Thunderbolt 5, buy a TB5 dock. If it does not, a TB4 dock is cheaper and you will not notice the difference today.

Watch two specs beyond bandwidth. Laptop charging wattage, because a 16-inch workstation can pull over 100 W and a 65 W dock will drain under load. And display outputs, because macOS limits how many external monitors a single Thunderbolt connection can drive, and the dock's port count does not change that.

CalDigit's TS5 Plus is the dock for people who want to never think about docks again. Twenty ports, 10 gigabit Ethernet, 140 W to the laptop, and Thunderbolt 5 bandwidth to run three 4K panels or two 6K. It wakes monitors from sleep reliably, which is rarer than it should be. CalDigit's firmware team actually ships updates.

It is big, it is the most expensive thing here, and it only shows its full ability with a Thunderbolt 5 host. If your laptop is TB4, buy the TS4 instead and save the difference.

The TS4 has been the dock to beat since it launched and nothing at the same price has done it. Eighteen ports, 2.5 gigabit Ethernet, 98 W charging, front-facing USB-C for the thing you plug in daily. It has survived three operating-system upgrades on our desks without a firmware drama.

The Mac display limit applies: one 6K or two 4K from a single Thunderbolt 4 cable. Windows machines can push more depending on the GPU.

Plugable's TBT4-UDZ is the pragmatic pick. It stands upright, so it takes the footprint of a paperback. It has two HDMI and two DisplayPort outputs, which means it covers whatever mix of monitors you already own without adapters. 100 W charging is enough for most laptops.

You give up a second downstream Thunderbolt port and the SD reader. For a lot of desks, that is nothing.

If you do not need a dock but you do have a laptop, a phone, a tablet and headphones all asking for power, Anker's 250 W desk charger replaces the pile of bricks under the desk with one block. Six ports, smart allocation that gives the laptop priority, and a small screen that shows live wattage so you can finally confirm which cable is slow.

The screen stops being interesting after a day. The price is high for a charger. It is still cheaper than the five chargers it replaces.

For the bag, UGREEN's 100 W Nexode is the one we keep buying. It is palm sized, the prongs fold, and it will charge a 14-inch laptop at full speed or a laptop and a phone together at a slight compromise. It costs less than a replacement brick from most laptop makers.

The limit is physics. 100 W shared across four ports means plugging in a phone slows the laptop a little. For travel that is fine.

## Cables matter

A dock is only as good as the cable in the box. For Thunderbolt 5 you need a cable rated for it, and anything over a metre should be active. For USB-C charging above 60 W, the cable must be marked for 100 W or 240 W. Cheap unmarked cables are the most common cause of "my dock is broken."
