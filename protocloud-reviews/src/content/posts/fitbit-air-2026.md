Fitbit Air is Google and Fitbit’s 2026 entry into the screenless wearable category. Instead of a smartwatch screen, it is a small pebble style tracker that stays focused on continuous health signals and sends insights to the Google Health app.

This guide explains what Fitbit Air does, which sensors it uses, how the Google Health app helps you interpret data, and what to consider if you want a simple tracker for sleep, recovery, and daily activity.

## What is Fitbit Air?

Fitbit Air is a screenless fitness and wellness tracker designed for comfortable 24/7 wear. The tracker is lightweight and intended to feel almost invisible during the day and at night.

Key idea: Fitbit Air tracks your data continuously, then you review trends and guidance inside the Google Health app.

## Fitbit Air sensors and what it tracks

Google describes Fitbit Air as a wearable that supports a core set of health signals:

- 24/7 heart rate and heart rate variability
- Heart rhythm monitoring with Afib alerts
- Blood oxygen monitoring (SpO2)
- Resting heart rate
- Skin temperature
- Sleep stages and sleep duration
- Automatic workout detection and workout recaps

Google Health features are presented in the app, including sleep and activity views.

## Fitbit Air and Google Health app

Fitbit Air pairs with the Google Health app for insights and coaching.

In 2026, Google is also consolidating the Fitbit app and Android Health Connect into a single Google Health experience. That matters because many features and your dashboard live inside Google Health rather than inside an older Fitbit app layout.

What you can expect inside the app:

- Sleep and recovery dashboards based on the Fitbit Air sensors
- Activity tracking and workout detection
- AI Health Coach style guidance that turns your metrics into practical next steps
- Guided workout flows you can start from your phone

Some advanced insights require Google Health Premium. If you care about full coaching and deeper recommendations, check what is included before you buy.

## Fitbit Air design: pebble, bands, and no screen

Fitbit Air is built as a modular screenless tracker.

From published product details, it supports:

- No screen or buttons on the tracker itself
- A small pebble that can be swapped between compatible bands
- Silent haptic alerts via a vibration motor

The removable pebble approach is designed for comfort and variety, so you can switch styles without replacing the core tracker.

## Battery life and charging speed

Google positions Fitbit Air with up to one week of battery life. It also includes fast charging, where a short charging session can provide a full day of power.

As always with wearables, battery life depends on settings and which features you use most, such as continuous monitoring and any app linked workouts.

## Water resistance and everyday use

The Verge reports Fitbit Air supports water resistance up to 50 meters. That makes it suitable for everyday activities like workouts and swimming sessions, based on the published rating.

If you plan to use it in saltwater or extreme conditions, check the product guidance for your specific use case.

## Privacy and medical accuracy note

Fitbit Air metrics are not intended to diagnose or treat medical conditions. Like all consumer health tracking, accuracy depends on your body, where the device sits, and your activity patterns.

Google also notes that features and eligibility can vary by country, and some capabilities require subscriptions and an internet connection.

If you have a specific health concern, use the device as a trend tool and follow up with a healthcare professional for clinical decisions.
