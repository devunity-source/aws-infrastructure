On a call, people form an opinion of you in the first few seconds and your camera does most of the work. Laptop webcams have improved but they still sit at the bottom of a thin lid with a sensor the size of a grain of rice. A dedicated webcam fixes the two things that matter most: light and detail. These five have replaced built-in cameras on our team.

## What makes a webcam good

**Sensor size beats resolution.** A 1080p camera with a large sensor will look better in a normal room than a 4K camera with a tiny one. Resolution only matters if your meeting app transmits it, and most compress to 720p or 1080p anyway.

**Low-light handling.** Home offices are rarely lit like studios. The best cameras keep noise down and skin tones natural when the only light is a window behind the monitor.

**Autofocus and framing.** You lean in, you lean back, you reach for coffee. A camera that keeps you sharp and centred without you noticing is doing its job.

**Privacy.** A physical shutter or a lens that points at the desk when idle is worth more than a software indicator.

Microphones on webcams are universally mediocre. If audio matters, read our [microphone guide](/best-usb-microphones-for-calls).

The Link 2 puts a 4K sensor on a small motorised gimbal. It finds your face, keeps you centred, and follows you if you get up to grab a notebook. Gesture controls let you zoom or lock framing with a raised hand. When you close your meeting app, the lens tilts down to face the desk, which is the clearest privacy signal any webcam offers.

The image is excellent in low light and the colour is natural without fiddling. If you sit still for every call, you are paying for a motor you will never use, and the MX Brio below does the same picture quality for less.

<ProductCard index={1} {...frontmatter.products[1]} />

Logitech's top fixed camera has a large sensor, good HDR, and a picture that looks right straight out of the box. Show Mode is the clever bit: tilt the camera down and it flips the image so you can show a sketch, a circuit board or a keyboard on your desk without unmounting anything.

It does not track. If you want to move around, buy the Link 2. For everyone else, this is the sensible 4K pick.

The Tadpole is the webcam that fits in a jacket pocket. It clips to the top of a laptop lid, weighs almost nothing, and produces an image that embarrasses the built-in camera next to it. The cable has a touch sensor that mutes the mic with a tap.

It is 1080p and the cable is short, so it lives on a laptop rather than a monitor. The software is best on a Mac. For coworking days, hotel rooms and client offices, it is the one we pack.

<ProductCard index={3} {...frontmatter.products[3]} />

If you want a clear upgrade without spending much, the Brio 505 is it. 1080p, auto-framing that keeps you centred, light correction that handles a dim room, and a shutter you can slide shut. Compared with a four-year-old laptop camera, the difference is night and day.

The image is softer than the 4K options and the build is plastic. For most daily calls none of that shows.

<ProductCard index={4} {...frontmatter.products[4]} />

Elgato built the Facecam MK.2 for streamers, which means it does things the others do not. Exposure, ISO and white balance are manual and stored in the camera, so your picture looks identical every day regardless of what the meeting app decides. Video is uncompressed at 1080p 60 fps.

There is no microphone, which is fine because you should not be using a webcam mic anyway. There is also no autofocus. Set the focus once for your seating position and it never hunts. That is either exactly what you want or a dealbreaker.

## Lighting matters more than the camera

The best webcam in a dark room looks worse than a cheap one with a window in front of you. Face a window if you can. If you cannot, a small light panel or a monitor light bar with a front-facing element does most of the work. Avoid light from behind or directly above.

- You travel: Opal Tadpole.
