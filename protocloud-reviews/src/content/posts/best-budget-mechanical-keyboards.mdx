A few years ago, a keyboard with hot-swap switches, a gasket mount and thick PBT keycaps was an enthusiast purchase. Now those features turn up on boards that cost less than a game. That is good news for anyone who games in the evening and writes code or email all day: a decent mechanical keyboard improves both. These five cover the range, from a wireless board we would happily use full-time to the cheapest one still worth buying. If you want to spend more, our [mechanical keyboards for programmers](/blog/best-mechanical-keyboards) guide has the premium picks.

## How we picked

We chose these from their specifications, the parts they use and what owners report, rather than a long hands-on test, and we say where the owner reviews are mixed. Every pick is a current model sold on Amazon.

**What matters most at this price:**

- **Hot-swap.** Sockets that let you pull a switch out and push another in, no soldering. If a switch fails or you want a different feel, it is a five-minute fix instead of a new keyboard. Four of the five boards here have it.
- **Keycaps.** PBT plastic keeps its texture for years. Cheaper ABS caps go shiny within months. Look for "PBT" and "double-shot" in the listing.
- **Mounting and dampening.** A gasket mount sits the plate on soft pads, and foam fills the empty space in the case. Together they turn a hollow clack into a softer, deeper sound. This is what separates the best budget boards from the rest.
- **Layout.** Full-size keeps everything. A 96% board keeps the number pad and arrows but packs them tighter, saving about two inches of desk for your mouse.

The F99 is one of the most popular budget enthusiast boards. It has a gasket mount, five layers of dampening, pre-lubed linear switches, PBT keycaps and hot-swap sockets, all the things that used to need a custom build. It is also wireless three ways: a 2.4 GHz dongle for gaming, Bluetooth for a laptop or tablet, and a USB-C cable when you want it. It remembers up to five devices.

The 8000 mAh battery is the biggest here by a distance, so you will charge it rarely. The 96% layout keeps the number pad and arrows while saving desk space. With thousands of reviews and a high average, it is also the safest bet in this guide. The case is plastic and the software is basic, but at this price those are easy trades.

<ProductCard index={1} {...frontmatter.products[1]} />

The R98 Pro gives you most of the F99's build without the wireless. It has the same 96% layout, a gasket mount with sound-absorbing foam, pre-lubed "cream" linear switches and hot-swap sockets. The thick PBT keycaps use the MDA profile, which is taller and more rounded than usual; some people love the cupped feel, others find it tall. There is a metal volume knob in the corner.

Owner reviews are more mixed than for the F99, and the complaints point to uneven quality control rather than a design problem. Buy it from a seller with easy returns and check every key on day one.

If you want every key, including a full number pad, and a big brand behind it, the Strix Scope II X is the pick. An aluminium top plate makes it feel solid, the PBT keycaps will last, and the hot-swap sockets come filled with ASUS's pre-lubed NX Snow linear switches. Dampening foam keeps the sound in check, a detachable wrist rest is in the box, and a wheel on the side handles volume and lighting.

It is wired and large, and ASUS's Armoury Crate software is heavier than it needs to be. You only need it to set lighting and macros once.

<ProductCard index={3} {...frontmatter.products[3]} />

The Apex 5 is here because it is hugely popular and well made, with a catch you should know about. Its "hybrid mechanical" switches put a clicky mechanism over a membrane, the rubber layer inside cheap office keyboards. They feel and sound clicky, but they are not true mechanical switches, they cannot be swapped and they wear differently.

What you get instead is extras. A small OLED screen shows game stats, Discord messages or any GIF you like. The aluminium frame is sturdy, and the magnetic wrist rest is a good one. If you want those and do not care what is under the keycaps, it is a good keyboard. If you came for mechanical switches, choose any other board here. The blue switches are also loud enough that your colleagues will hear them on calls.

The K668 is one of the least expensive mechanical keyboards you can buy, and it still has hot-swap sockets, quiet red linear switches, four dedicated hotkeys for mute, calculator, lock and desktop, and a second set of keycaps in the box.

It feels lighter and more hollow than the boards above, and owner reviews are mixed. As a first mechanical keyboard, or a spare for a second desk, it does the job, and its hot-swap sockets mean you can upgrade the switches later.

## Upgrades that cost almost nothing

- **Swap the switches.** Every board here except the Apex 5 takes standard 3 and 5-pin switches. A set of 100 tactile or silent switches costs little and changes the feel completely.
- **A foam or tape mod.** Painter's tape on the back of the circuit board deepens the sound of hollow boards like the K668. Look up the "tape mod" before trying it.
- **A desk mat.** It absorbs sound from below and stops the keyboard sliding.
