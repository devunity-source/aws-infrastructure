A lot of engineers have two mice: a heavy, button-laden productivity mouse by day and a light gaming mouse by night. That is one mouse too many. Modern gaming mice are light enough to be comfortable through a full workday, and a few have the extra controls that make editor and browser work faster. These four do both jobs well. If you never game, our [productivity mouse guide](/best-mice-for-productivity) has the ergonomic picks.

## What crosses over

**Weight.** The best thing gaming did for mice was make them light. A 60 g mouse causes less wrist fatigue over a day than a 140 g one, full stop. This benefits everyone.

**Sensor and latency.** Any mouse here tracks flawlessly and responds instantly. For work, that shows up as a cursor that goes exactly where you meant, every time.

**Battery.** Gaming mice went from charging nightly to charging monthly. Wireless is now the default.

**Buttons.** This is where the two uses diverge. Competitive mice strip buttons for weight. Productivity wants a thumb wheel and a few extras. Only one mouse here really has them.

<ProductCard index={0} {...frontmatter.products[0]} />

The Superlight 2 is the mouse half the professional gaming scene uses and it turns out that makes it an excellent office mouse. At around 60 grams your wrist barely notices it. The shape is a safe medium that suits palm and claw grips. The battery lasts weeks, and the wireless is as reliable as a cable.

What you give up is buttons. Two side buttons, a scroll wheel, that is it. No thumb wheel for horizontal scroll, no free-spin for long files. If you lean on those, look at the G502 X Plus. If you do not, this is the one mouse to own.

<ProductCard index={1} {...frontmatter.products[1]} />

The Viper V3 Pro is a few grams lighter still and has a shape that fingertip and claw grippers tend to prefer: lower, flatter, with a gentle hump at the back. Tracking is perfect, battery is long, and the symmetric body feels balanced.

Side buttons are on the left only, so it is right-handed in practice despite the shape. Razer's software has little to offer for productivity. As a pure pointing device for all day and all evening, it is superb.

The G502 X Plus is the one gaming mouse that is also a serious productivity mouse. Thirteen programmable controls, including a thumb-side sniper button that is ideal for push-to-talk or a modifier, and the scroll wheel toggles between notched and free-spin for flying through a long log. The sculpted shape fills a palm grip comfortably.

It is heavy by current standards. Over 100 grams is fine for most people and tiring for some. It is right-handed only and comes with lighting you will switch off. If you want buttons, nothing else here comes close.

<ProductCard index={3} {...frontmatter.products[3]} />

Lamzu made its name selling flagship-grade mice for far less, and the Atlantis Mini Pro is the one we recommend. Around 50 grams, a top-tier PixArt sensor, solid wireless and a comfortable symmetrical shape. For the money it is remarkable.

The software is basic and the Mini size suits medium hands best. Larger hands should look at the standard Atlantis. The shell has small cutouts that some people like and some do not.

## A note on mouse pads

A light mouse on a bare desk or a tiny pad is wasted. A large cloth pad gives consistent glide and lets you lower your sensitivity, which improves precision for both code and games. It is the cheapest upgrade in this entire category.

## The short version

- One mouse for everything: Logitech G Pro X Superlight 2.
- Claw or fingertip grip: Razer Viper V3 Pro.
- You want buttons and a free-spin wheel: Logitech G502 X Plus.
- Flagship feel on a budget: Lamzu Atlantis Mini Pro.
