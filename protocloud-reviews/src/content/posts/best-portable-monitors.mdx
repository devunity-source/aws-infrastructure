A second screen is the productivity upgrade most people cannot give up once they have it, and then they travel and lose it. Portable monitors solve that. A good one is a single USB-C cable, weighs less than a hardback book, and turns a hotel desk or a client's spare meeting room into a workable setup. These four are the ones that held up.

## What to look for

**Single-cable USB-C.** The laptop supplies both video and power over one cable. Check that your laptop's USB-C port supports DisplayPort Alt Mode, which almost every laptop from the last five years does.

**Weight and stand.** The monitor is only portable if you actually pack it. Under 700 grams is the target, and a stand that does not collapse when you nudge the desk matters more than you think.

**Panel size.** 14 to 16 inches matches most laptops and fits in the same sleeve. Larger than that stops being portable.

**Brightness.** 300 nits minimum. Cafes and trains have more ambient light than your office.

OLED in a portable monitor sounds like overkill until you use one next to an IPS panel. Black is black, colour is rich, and text on a dark editor theme looks printed. The ZenScreen is light, powers from one cable, and the folding cover props it up in landscape or portrait.

It is 1080p, which at 15.6 inches is fine for a secondary screen holding Slack and a terminal, less so for a main editor. The glossy finish reflects in bright rooms. Static toolbars for hours every day carry some burn-in risk, so vary what you put on it.

<ProductCard index={1} {...frontmatter.products[1]} />

Lenovo's M14t does one thing no other panel here does: it passes power through. Plug your charger into the monitor and the monitor into the laptop, and both charge off one brick. For business travellers that removes a cable and a plug from the bag.

The 2240 by 1400 panel is sharp, the hinge is a proper stand rather than a folding cover, and touch plus pen work on Windows and most Linux desktops. IPS contrast is average and the price is high for a 14-inch panel, but the engineering is the most thought-through in the category.

The Espresso 15 Pro is the portable monitor built for people who own a MacBook and want the second screen to feel like it came from the same factory. Thin aluminium, a 4K panel that is the sharpest here, and touch that works on macOS through Espresso's app, which almost nothing else manages.

It is expensive, and the clever magnetic stand costs extra on top. It also wants a fair amount of power over USB-C, so pair it with a laptop that can deliver it or use the included power path.

<ProductCard index={3} {...frontmatter.products[3]} />

If you want a second screen for the price of a nice dinner, the ARZOPA Z1FC is a 16-inch 1080p panel with a built-in kickstand and a 144 Hz refresh that makes scrolling smooth. It is bright enough for a cafe window and it just works over USB-C or HDMI.

It is heavier than the premium options and the colour is nothing special. For a second terminal or a Slack window on the road, it does the job for very little money.

## Portrait mode is underrated

Every monitor here supports portrait orientation. A 15-inch panel turned on its side holds a long log file, a chat sidebar or a documentation page with far less scrolling than landscape. Most people never try it. Try it.

## The short version

- Best picture in a bag: ASUS ZenScreen OLED.
- Windows laptop, want power passthrough and touch: Lenovo M14t Gen 2.
- MacBook owner, money is secondary: Espresso 15 Pro.
- Cheap and cheerful: ARZOPA Z1FC.
