"Smart glasses" now covers three quite different products. Some are ordinary-looking frames with a camera, speakers and an AI assistant you talk to. Some add a small display inside the lens for messages, directions and captions. And some are effectively a monitor you wear: plug them into a laptop and you get a large private screen on a train or a plane. This guide covers the best of each, and helps you work out which kind you want.

## How we picked

These picks come from manufacturer specifications, professional reviews and owner reports. We have not tested these glasses ourselves. Prices are left out because they change often; the buttons go to the live listing.

The market moved again at Meta's Connect event on September 23, 2026, with a third-generation Ray-Ban Meta. Google and Samsung's Android XR glasses have been announced but are not on sale yet. We cover both below.

## Which kind do you need?

**Camera and audio glasses** are the most natural to wear. They take photos and video, play music and calls through open-ear speakers, and answer questions through an AI assistant. There is no screen. Battery life covers most of a day.

**Display glasses** add a small heads-up display to one or both lenses: notifications, navigation, captions of the person talking to you, translation. They are newer, heavier on battery, and the software is still young.

**Display or "XR" glasses** are a different thing again. They have no battery or assistant. You plug them into a laptop, phone or games console over USB-C and see a large virtual screen. They are for sitting down, not for wearing around town.

**Prescriptions.** Ranges vary a lot, from -4 to +4 on the Meta Display to -12 to +12 on the Even G2. Check yours before you fall for a frame.

**Privacy.** Glasses with cameras have a light that shows when they are recording, but people around you may still be uncomfortable, especially at work. If you will wear them in meetings, a camera-free pair avoids the question.

**Where you live.** Meta's AI features are officially supported in a limited list of countries, mostly North America, the UK, Australia and Western Europe, and EU users get a restricted assistant. Check Meta's list before buying for the AI.

The Ray-Ban Meta is the pair most people should start with, because it looks like a normal pair of Ray-Bans. A 12-megapixel camera in the corner shoots photos and 3K video, open-ear speakers handle calls, music and podcasts, and you can ask Meta AI about what you are looking at. Battery life is up to eight hours, with two more days of charging in the case. Reviewers consistently describe them as light and comfortable enough to forget you are wearing them.

The weak points are software and trust. Photos are portrait only, the AI features impress some people and leave others cold, and you have to run everything through the Meta AI app, which raises data-sharing questions for some owners.

Meta launched the third generation on September 23, 2026, with a 12 MP camera, up to nine hours of battery, a new six-microphone array and slimmer frames. It is sold by Meta and eyewear stores rather than Amazon for now, and early hands-on reviews describe it as a modest upgrade. If you already own Gen 2, there is no rush. If you are buying fresh, either is a good choice.

<ProductCard index={1} {...frontmatter.products[1]} />

The Meta Ray-Ban Display is the first mainstream glasses with a proper full-colour display. A 600 by 600 pixel screen sits in the right lens and shows messages, turn-by-turn directions, live captions and translation, and a viewfinder for the camera. It is bright enough to read outdoors, and Engadget found it clear without being distracting.

You control it with the Meta Neural Band, a wristband that reads tiny finger movements through the muscles in your wrist. Reviewers say it is uncannily accurate, and it means you can reply to a message without touching your glasses or talking out loud.

It is a first generation product. The frames are noticeably chunkier than the Ray-Ban Meta, the app catalogue is small, messaging is limited on iPhone, and heavy display use eats the battery. Prescriptions are limited to -4 to +4. It is sold by Meta and retail partners, not Amazon, and getting the size right matters, so use Meta's sizing tool or try a pair in a store.

The Even G2 takes the opposite approach to Meta. There is no camera and no speaker, just a green text display in both lenses and a very light frame that passes for ordinary glasses. That makes it the easiest pair to wear in an office: nobody wonders whether they are being recorded.

The software is aimed at work. A teleprompter scrolls your notes as you speak, Conversate transcribes meetings, translation covers dozens of languages, and an AI assistant answers questions on the display. Battery life is about two days, and prescriptions run from -12 to +12, by far the widest range here, so it is the pick for strong prescriptions.

Reviewers find the touch controls awkward and the optional R1 ring disappointing, and the app ecosystem is small and occasionally glitchy. Even Realities sells the G2 directly; the Amazon listing was unavailable when we checked.

<ProductCard index={3} {...frontmatter.products[3]} />

If what you want is a big screen on the move, display glasses are the answer, and the VITURE Beast is our pick. Two Sony micro-OLED panels give each eye a 1920 by 1200 picture at 120 Hz, across a 58-degree field of view, which is the widest and brightest in its class. Built-in tracking can pin the screen in place as you move your head, or stretch it into an ultrawide.

Plug it into a laptop with a USB-C port that outputs video and it appears as an extra monitor. That makes it a useful way to code on a train or a plane with a second screen nobody else can see. The build is premium, and electronic dimming turns the lenses dark so the picture is easier to see.

VITURE's companion software has a rocky reputation on Windows, prescription inserts cost extra, and it is heavy enough to notice after an hour or two.

<ProductCard index={4} {...frontmatter.products[4]} />

The XREAL One Pro is the Beast's closest rival and the one to pick if you work across several virtual screens. XREAL's X1 chip keeps pinned screens rock steady with very little lag, and the flat-prism optics keep text sharp right to the edges. It works with nearly anything that outputs video over USB-C, from a MacBook to a Steam Deck.

It is dimmer than the Beast, runs warm, and its best extras, like the XREAL Eye camera for full spatial tracking, cost more. On Amazon it is sold through third-party offers rather than XREAL directly, so check the seller.

<ProductCard index={5} {...frontmatter.products[5]} />

The VITURE Pro 2 is the value pick for display glasses, and the one for short-sighted wearers. Built-in dials correct short sight down to -5.0, so many people can skip prescription inserts entirely. At 63 grams it is the lightest display glasses here, and reviewers praise its sharpness for text-heavy work like code and spreadsheets.

There is no electronic dimming, so you get a clip-on shade instead, and owners notice the right arm getting warm. The dials do not help with astigmatism or strong short sight.

## Worth waiting for?

- **Ray-Ban Meta Audio** drops the camera but keeps the speakers and assistant, with longer battery life. It ships on October 13, 2026, and is worth a look if you want Meta's glasses in an office without a camera.
- **Android XR glasses** from Google and Samsung, in Warby Parker and Gentle Monster frames with Gemini built in, have been announced but are not on sale yet. Reports point to late 2026.

If you are on the fence about display glasses for work, a real second screen is still cheaper and kinder on your eyes; see our [portable monitor guide](/best-portable-monitors). And if you want health tracking more than a camera or a screen, a screenless tracker like [Fitbit Air](/fitbit-air-2026) or the [Oura ring](/oura-ring-ai-health-monitoring-ring-5) does that job better.

## The short version

- AI glasses most people will enjoy: Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 2), or Gen 3 if you are buying new.
- A real display in the lens: Meta Ray-Ban Display.
- Meetings, privacy and strong prescriptions: Even Realities G2.
- The biggest private laptop screen: VITURE Beast.
- Several pinned screens with low lag: XREAL One Pro.
- Display glasses on a budget, or if you are short-sighted: VITURE Pro 2.
