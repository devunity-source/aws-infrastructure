The dream desk has one cable. You sit down, plug a single connector into the laptop, and the monitors, keyboard, Ethernet, webcam and charger all light up. In practice, the market is full of docks that drop a display after sleep or charge the laptop too slowly to keep up with a build. These are the ones we would buy, with the display limits spelled out, because that is where most docks disappoint.

## Thunderbolt 4 or 5?

Thunderbolt 4 at 40 Gbps is plenty for two 4K displays, 2.5 gigabit Ethernet and a handful of peripherals. Thunderbolt 5 doubles that, which leaves room for more and bigger monitors plus faster external SSDs, if the laptop's chip can drive them. If your laptop has Thunderbolt 5, buy a TB5 dock. If it does not, a TB4 dock is cheaper and you will not notice the difference today.

Watch two specs beyond bandwidth. Laptop charging wattage, because a 16-inch workstation can pull over 100 W and a 65 W dock will drain under load. And display support, because the laptop's chip sets how many external monitors you can run, and the dock's port count does not change that. Base M1 and M2 Macs drive one external display, most newer Macs two, and only the M5 Pro and M5 Max go beyond that.

CalDigit's TS5 Plus has twenty ports, including two downstream Thunderbolt 5 ports, 10 gigabit Ethernet, SD and microSD readers, and 140 W to the laptop. CalDigit publishes exactly which chips get how many displays: two on most Macs, three on an M5 Pro, four on an M5 Max, and two on Windows, or three on some Thunderbolt 5 PCs.

It is big, at 0.9 kg, it is the most expensive thing here, and it only shows its full ability with a Thunderbolt 5 host. If your laptop is TB4, buy the TS4 instead and save the difference.

<ProductCard index={1} {...frontmatter.products[1]} />

The TS4 has been the reference Thunderbolt 4 dock since it launched. It has eighteen ports, including two downstream Thunderbolt 4 ports, 2.5 gigabit Ethernet, 98 W charging, and front-facing SD and microSD readers next to the USB-C port you plug in daily.

It drives two displays on most Macs and Thunderbolt Windows PCs, and one on base M1 and M2 Macs. Two monitors run at up to 4K 60 Hz, or 6K on an M1 Pro, Max or Ultra.

Plugable's TBT4-UDZ is the pick for Windows desks with a lot of screens. It has two HDMI and two DisplayPort outputs and runs up to four 4K 60 Hz monitors from a Thunderbolt 4 Windows laptop, without adapters. It stands upright on an included stand or lies flat, and delivers 98 W to the laptop, which covers most of them.

Three catches. There are no downstream Thunderbolt ports, the bandwidth goes to the displays. Macs still get two displays at most, and base M1 and M2 Macs one. And the DisplayPort 1.2 outputs can't drive a 5K or 6K monitor. On a Mac, the TS4 is the better buy.

If you do not need a dock but you do have a laptop, a phone, a tablet and headphones all asking for power, Anker's 250 W desk charger replaces the pile of bricks under the desk with one block. Four USB-C and two USB-A ports, one of them good for 140 W, and a small screen that shows live wattage so you can finally confirm which cable is slow.

The screen stops being interesting after a day. The price is high for a charger. It is still cheaper than the five chargers it replaces.

For the bag, we would pick UGREEN's 100 W Nexode. It is palm sized, the prongs fold on the US model, and one USB-C port delivers up to 100 W, enough to charge a 14-inch laptop at full speed, or a laptop and a phone together at a slight compromise. It costs less than a replacement brick from most laptop makers.

The catch is that the 100 W is shared across four ports, so plugging in a phone slows the laptop a little. For travel that is fine.

## Cables matter

A dock is only as good as the cable in the box. For Thunderbolt 5 you need a cable rated for it, and anything over a metre should be active. For USB-C charging above 60 W, the cable must be marked for 100 W or 240 W. Cheap unmarked cables are a common cause of "my dock is broken."
